## Part 3: Four Frameworks

Before writing your argument, consider how each of the following frameworks would handle your verdicts.

| Framework | Core claim | Implies permissible if… |
|-----------|-----------|------------------------|
| **Formalism** | An act is permissible if and only if the written rules allow it. Morality in sport is fully captured by the rulebook. | No rule is broken, regardless of intent or effect. |
| **Internalism** *(Suits, Morgan)* | Rules serve the game's internal goods — the point of the contest. An act that is technically legal but subverts what the game is *for* is still a form of cheating. | The act is consistent with the game's constitutive purpose. |
| **Broad internalism** *(D'Agostino)* | Beyond written rules sits the "ethos of the game" — the informal norms players actually share. Legitimacy is judged against this shared understanding. | The act would be accepted as legitimate by most participants in that sport's community. |
| **Kantian universalisability** | Ask whether your maxim could be universalised. If every player adopted your tactic and the game thereby ceased to function or ceased to be worth playing, the tactic is impermissible. | You could will that all players act the same way without undermining the game itself. |
